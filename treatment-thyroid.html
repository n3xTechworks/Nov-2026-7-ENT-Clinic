Thyroidectomy

Removal of part or all of the thyroid gland through a small neck crease incision, with nerve monitoring to protect your voice.

Duration1.5–3 hours
AnaesthesiaGeneral
Hospital stay2 nights
Back to work2 weeks
InsuranceCashless available

Who it is for

  • Thyroid nodules that are suspicious or cancerous on FNAC
  • Large goitre causing pressure, breathing or swallowing trouble
  • Overactive thyroid not controlled with medicines
  • Nodules that keep growing on follow-up scans

How it is done

Before surgery

Ultrasound, FNAC, thyroid blood tests and a vocal cord check with a laryngoscope.

On the day

Through a 4–6 cm crease incision, half or all of the gland is removed using nerve monitoring.

Hospital stay

Calcium levels and voice are checked; most patients go home on day 2.

Follow-up

Wound check at 1 week, histology results and thyroid hormone plan at 2 weeks.

Risks and safety

  • Temporary hoarseness in about 5%; permanent voice change is rare (under 1%) with nerve monitoring
  • Low calcium after total thyroidectomy, usually temporary and treated with tablets
  • Bleeding in the neck in the first 24 hours (1–2%), which is why you stay in
  • Lifelong thyroid tablets after total thyroidectomy

Your specialist goes through the risks that apply to you before you sign a consent form. Ask as many questions as you need.

Common questions

Will I need thyroid tablets?

After total thyroidectomy, yes, a daily tablet for life. After half-thyroidectomy, about 1 in 4 people need one.

How visible is the scar?

The cut is placed in a natural neck crease and usually fades to a fine line within a year.

Will my voice change?

We monitor the voice nerves throughout surgery. Most people have a normal voice; mild tiredness of the voice for a few weeks is common.

Is it covered by insurance?

Yes, as an inpatient procedure. Our desk arranges cashless approval.

Preparing for surgery

Thyroidectomy

Tests to complete, medicines to adjust and what to pack for two nights.

  • Nothing to eat for 6 hours and no clear fluids for 2 hours before your admission time.
  • Ask your physician before pausing aspirin, clopidogrel or other blood thinners; we tell you exactly when.
  • Bring your scans, blood reports and a written list of all your medicines.
  • Bring photo ID and your insurance card so our desk can arrange cashless approval.
  • Arrange an adult to take you home and stay with you for the first night.
  • Pack loose clothes, toiletries and a phone charger for your stay.
